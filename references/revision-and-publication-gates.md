# Revision and publication gates

Use these gates after the architecture exists and again after the full draft. Record findings with location, evidence, impact, recommendation, confidence, and disposition.

## Gate order

1. **Argument:** one controlling claim; no hidden second thesis.
2. **Contribution:** the reader update is specific and supported.
3. **Evidence:** material claims, quotes, numbers, dates, and attributions are verified; claim strength matches design and source.
4. **Counterargument:** the strongest credible objection is represented fairly and changes the reasoning where it should.
5. **Structure:** opening, thesis, evidence ladder, turn, Ask, and close form one movement; each paragraph earns its place.
6. **Voice:** authorial vantage is visible; the prose is not a generic public-intellectual register or an imitation.
7. **Texture:** concrete details are source-traceable, distributed with purpose, and not used beyond scope.
8. **Line/craft:** actors, verbs, rhythm, paragraph endings, compression, and read-aloud quality are deliberate.
9. **Anti-slop:** no canned openings, generic filler, default symmetry, false balance, borrowed mannerisms, invented texture, or inflated close.
10. **Headline/opening:** the public promise is clear, accurate, specific, and paid off.
11. **Outlet fit:** audience, length, peg, timing, exclusivity, author authority, conflicts, links, and format are current and recorded.
12. **Ethics/legal:** no fabrication, plagiarism, material omission, undisclosed conflict, privacy breach, coercive rhetoric, or unsupported allegation.

## Hard stop conditions

Do not mark `ADOPT` when any of these remains unresolved:

- a material claim is `UNVERIFIED`, `CONTESTED`, or stronger than its evidence;
- the piece is clear but interchangeable;
- vividness depends on invented or undisclosed composite material;
- the headline promises a different or larger piece;
- the conclusion makes a stronger claim than the body;
- the writer’s authority or conflict is material and unrecorded;
- current venue requirements are unknown for a real submission;
- the review is presented as independent when it is not.

## Cold-read protocol

Give the draft without the brief to a fresh reviewer if possible. Ask:

- What is the piece about?
- What does it argue?
- What evidence or experience makes that argument credible?
- What would the writer want the reader to think or do?
- Which sentence or detail remains?
- What feels generic, overstated, or unearned?

Then show the brief and compare. If the cold reader’s account differs materially, classify the result as `FRAMEWORK_MISMATCH` or `DRIFT_REQUIRES_DECISION`; do not merely polish the prose.
