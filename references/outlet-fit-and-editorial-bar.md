# Outlet fit and editorial bar

Research snapshot: 2026-08-14. These are public editorial benchmarks, not guarantees of acceptance or permission to imitate a publication’s house style. Submission rules are time-sensitive; verify them again before submitting.

## The shared bar

Across strong opinion pages, the recurring requirements are a clear argument, specific evidence, a fresh or useful perspective, a credible author relationship to the subject, readable prose, and a reason the piece matters now. The skill treats those as quality dimensions, not as a formula. A target venue still requires its own length, timing, exclusivity, conflict, contact, and formatting checks.

## Public guidance by venue

### The Washington Post

The Post’s [op-ed submission guidance](https://helpcenter.washingtonpost.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003675788-Submit-an-op-ed) asks for a clear point of view or argument supported by specific evidence, a completed article, full contact information, and disclosure of personal or financial interests. Its [writing guide](https://www.washingtonpost.com/opinions/2022/op-ed-writing-guide-washington-post-examples/?itid=sn_letters) emphasizes a focused piece, an easily located thesis/main argument, and a short takeaway that could survive as a headline. The public guidance lists 750–800 words as an ideal range and says the work should deepen understanding, explain meaning, equip readers, change perspective, expose a topic, or articulate a distinctive view.

**Skill translation:** make the argument easy to find; give the reader an interpretable consequence; treat freshness and timeliness as part of the brief; do not confuse a personal essay, investigation, or institutional promotion with an op-ed.

### The New York Times

The [Opinion Guest Essays guidance](https://thenewyorktimeshelpcenter.helpjuice.com/115014809107-New-York-Times-Opinion-Guest-Essays) describes a guest essay as an argument delivered in the author’s voice, grounded in fact, expertise, or experience, and connected to a matter of broad public concern. It expects key assertions to be supported with sources, hyperlinks, or parenthetical references and does not treat unverifiable facts as publishable support.

**Skill translation:** authorial voice and evidence are co-requirements. Establish why this writer can make this argument, and make the factual scaffolding inspectable.

### The Los Angeles Times

The [Los Angeles Times opinion submission page](https://www.latimes.com/oe-howtosubmitoped-story.html) publicly describes opinion submissions as exclusive, completed drafts rather than partial pitches, with a stated 750–1,000-word range and author contact/biographical information.

**Skill translation:** draft to the requested form, preserve a timely public argument, and record exclusivity and bio requirements in the editorial brief.

### The Hechinger Report

The [Hechinger Report op-ed guidelines](https://hechingerreport.org/op-ed-guidelines/) emphasize education inequality and innovation, research/data/stories, voices not always heard, an argument about ideas or policy, accessible and jargon-free writing, a stand-alone piece, and a length under 900 words.

**Skill translation:** translate expertise for a broad education audience; use evidence and lived consequences without turning the piece into a literature review; check whose voice is missing or overrepresented.

### Forbes

[Forbes Editorial Values and Standards](https://www.forbes.com/sites/forbesstaff/article/forbes-editorial-values-and-standards//) emphasizes independence, integrity, fairness, factual authority, inclusion, conflict disclosure, and avoiding distortion, exaggeration, sensationalism, and carelessness. Forbes also has distinct contributor networks and editorial processes; do not assume that “Forbes” names one uniform format or acceptance path.

**Skill translation:** distinguish outlet reputation from contributor pathway. Check conflicts, attribution, originality, disclosure, factual authority, and whether the argument serves readers rather than the author’s promotion.

### The Financial Times

The Financial Times’ public opinion-submission page, [“Want to write a piece for the Financial Times opinion page?”](https://www.ft.com/content/e3e8ff2b-95c7-48f8-9eda-312494422e10), is currently partly subscription-gated, so its current submission details must be rechecked through the live page. The public title and accessible editorial guidance emphasize original/exclusive, punchy, readable arguments, clear wording, and the value of challenging conventional wisdom without hiding behind jargon.

**Skill translation:** aim for a precise claim with intellectual bite and economical wording; make dissent useful rather than performative; never confuse compression with omission of necessary evidence or limits.

### The Chicago Tribune

The Chicago Tribune’s current public submission rules should be verified directly before use. A [Tribune staff explanation of its opinion pages](https://www.yahoo.com/news/read-opinions-pages-guide-different-213000672.html) describes the pages as a platform for dialogue, diverse views, and commentary intended to arouse interest, provoke thought, and encourage discussion. Because the accessible source is secondary, treat this as a directional benchmark, not a current house rule.

**Skill translation:** write for civic conversation: make the disagreement legible, avoid contempt, and give readers something more useful than a position signal.

## Additional practice anchors

[Stanford GSE’s op-ed guide](https://ed.stanford.edu/resources/faculty/write-pitch-op-eds) adds audience/action, timing, outlet fit, a new contribution, personal voice, evidence/links, solutions, and preempting critics. [Georgetown’s op-ed resource](https://library.georgetown.edu/scholarly-communication/faculty-media-op-eds) similarly emphasizes a fresh viewpoint, relevant expertise, a timely peg, plain English, an early thesis, fact checking, and acknowledgment of opposing views.

## Do not imitate

Do not ask for “a New York Times voice,” “a Financial Times voice,” or the voice of a living columnist. Convert the request into high-level, transferable properties: argument clarity, public relevance, evidence discipline, readable compression, intellectual independence, tonal range, and a distinctive authorial vantage. The voiceprint must be generated from the writer and the corpus.

## Outlet-fit checklist

Before `ADOPT`, record:

- target audience and section;
- current word-count and submission rules with access date;
- timely peg and why-now sentence;
- author authority, biography, conflict, and disclosure needs;
- exclusivity or duplicate-publication status;
- source/link/citation expectations;
- whether the draft is a full piece, pitch, or query;
- the strongest reason the venue’s readers—not merely the author—should care;
- a fallback venue or a decision to defer if fit is uncertain.
