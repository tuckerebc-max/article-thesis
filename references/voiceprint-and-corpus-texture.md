# Voiceprint and corpus texture

Voice is the pattern of attention and judgment that makes a piece recognizably this writer’s work. It cannot be added reliably by asking for “more personality.” It emerges from a specific vantage, a specific corpus, and repeated choices about what to notice, name, connect, doubt, and refuse.

## Voiceprint card

```markdown
# Voiceprint

- Writer / project:
- Subject and audience:
- Vantage: where the writer stands in relation to the issue:
- Authority: what the writer knows, saw, did, studied, or cannot claim:
- Felt stakes:
- Ethical center: what the piece refuses to trivialize, exaggerate, or hide:
- Intellectual posture:
- Diction: plain / technical / regional / institutional / mixed:
- Rhythm: sentence-length range, paragraph movement, fragments, questions:
- First-person boundary and disclosure:
- Tonal range and earned transitions:
- Image ecology: concrete objects, places, or terms that belong to this subject:
- Distinctive phrases/passages to protect:
- Clichés, abstractions, filler, and borrowed mannerisms to avoid:
- Uncertainty signature: how the writer names limits without flattening the argument:
```

Do not fill a voiceprint with adjectives alone. Support each major choice with a source, prior writing sample, observed speech pattern, project constraint, or deliberate decision. A writer can be wry without making every paragraph witty; urgent without inflating every consequence; personal without making the piece autobiographical.

## Corpus texture bank

Texture is not decoration. It is the concrete grain through which an abstract claim becomes inspectable. Record each item with provenance and function:

| Texture ID | Detail | Source/provenance | What it shows | Scope/limitation | Planned use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| X-01 |  |  |  |  | opening / evidence / turn / close |

Useful texture includes exact language, a procedural detail, a named place, a material object, a date, a measured number with denominator, a contradiction between policy and practice, an ordinary consequence, or a human voice that the writer has permission to use. Never invent sensory detail to make a sourced event feel cinematic.

## Craft-mode palette

Choose one dominant mode and at most one supporting mode unless the architecture explicitly requires more:

- scene or portrait;
- explanatory/causal;
- moral or policy argument;
- historical or comparative;
- systems or institutional analysis;
- personal testimony or witnessed account;
- data-led argument;
- reversal or contrarian diagnosis;
- reported case.

Use mode changes to mark a change in argumentative job. A scene can establish stakes; a number can bound them; a counterexample can pressure a generalization; a historical turn can alter the frame. Do not alternate modes merely to avoid boredom.

## Distinctiveness tests

- **Paste test:** could this paragraph be inserted into a different writer’s piece with only nouns changed? If yes, make the observation, judgment, or language more particular.
- **Source-return test:** can every vivid factual detail be traced back to the corpus or clearly marked as the writer’s interpretation?
- **Moral pressure test:** is the sentence’s force earned by what is at stake, or by inflated adjectives and abstract urgency?
- **Voice/polish test:** if grammar were made perfectly conventional, would the writer’s way of seeing remain?
- **Read-aloud test:** do sentence lengths, stresses, and paragraph endings create deliberate movement rather than a uniform AI cadence?

Protect roughness when it is intentional and truthful. Remove roughness that is accidental, vague, or evasive. Do not smooth away a writer’s distinctive syntax merely because a generic editor would choose a more regular sentence.
