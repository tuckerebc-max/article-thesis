# Hook and headline craft

This reference handles the compressed public-facing layer after a thesis candidate is visible. It treats attention as a service to the reader: the entry point should make the right promise, quickly and honestly, then the piece should deliver more than the promise without changing the subject.

## Keep the layers distinct

- **Thesis:** what the piece argues.
- **Hook:** how the piece enters the argument.
- **Headline/title:** what the reader is promised before entering.
- **Lead/opening:** the first delivered experience.
- **Payoff:** where the thesis is demonstrated, qualified, or applied.

A hook can be emotionally vivid while the thesis is analytical. A headline can be playful while the thesis is serious. The headline and hook must nevertheless be traceable to the thesis and evidence map.

## Hook card

```markdown
## H-01 — short name

- Thesis ID:
- Hook type:
- Exact source material / provenance:
- What the reader sees, hears, or learns first:
- Immediate question or tension:
- Promise made:
- Beat where the promise is paid off:
- Thesis connection:
- Evidence and qualifier:
- Audience fit:
- Ethical / accuracy risks:
- Headline variants:
- Rejection reason if not selected:
```

Use `DIRECT`, `PARAPHRASE`, `INFERRED`, `HYPOTHETICAL`, or `NEEDS-SOURCE` on the source-material line. If the hook is reconstructed from a real person or event, do not invent dialogue, interiority, chronology, or sensory detail. If the hook is hypothetical, label it before the reader could mistake it for reportage.

## Hook families and guardrails

| Hook family | Best when | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Concrete case or scene | A bounded person, decision, or event makes an abstract claim visible | Do not generalize from the case without a separate evidence bridge |
| Contradiction or anomaly | The evidence genuinely conflicts with the audience’s expectation | Do not manufacture a paradox by omitting context |
| Decision first | The audience must understand a choice, trade-off, or consequence | Show who has authority and what is uncertain |
| Significant finding | A result changes how the audience should see the problem | Include population, date, design, effect/uncertainty, and limitation |
| Question | The piece can answer a real question and the answer matters | Do not use a rhetorical question to hide that no answer is available |
| Reversal | A familiar interpretation becomes inadequate | State the old frame fairly before replacing it |
| Future projection | The decision has plausible alternative futures | Label scenarios and uncertainties; never present projection as fact |
| Shared condition | The audience recognizes the problem or decision | Do not presume a shared identity, emotion, or experience |
| Image/object | A concrete artifact embodies the argument | Explain its relevance; do not use atmosphere as proof |

Choose one dominant opening mode. Layering several hooks at once usually signals that the thesis has not been chosen.

## Headline as a reader promise

A headline/title should normally communicate one main idea. It can be a sentence, question, phrase, or creative line, but a reader who encounters it alone should be able to form a fair expectation of the subject and value of the piece.

Use the following construction sequence:

1. Write a plain thesis-to-headline translation with no cleverness.
2. Write an explanatory version that names the problem, mechanism, or consequence.
3. Write a concrete or human version connected to the same thesis.
4. Write a tension or question version only if it does not withhold a material fact.
5. Compare all variants against the headline gate and choose the clearest version that still has life.

The headline may not claim a stronger causal relationship, wider population, more certain result, or larger recommendation than the body. If the headline is deliberately narrower than the body, confirm that it still names the piece’s true point rather than a colorful entry point.

## Headline integrity checklist

Run each candidate through these questions and record `PASS`, `REVISE`, or `FAIL` with evidence.

### Accuracy and scope

- Does each noun, verb, number, comparison, and modifier match the source material?
- Does the tense match what happened and when?
- Does the headline imply causation, certainty, scale, or blame that the evidence does not support?
- Does it distinguish a finding from a claim, allegation, proposal, prediction, or opinion?

### Out-of-context comprehension

- Would an unfamiliar reader know the topic without the site name, image, subhead, or surrounding story?
- Are names, acronyms, pronouns, and metaphors interpretable to the intended audience?
- Does the headline still make sense in a search result, social feed, email, slide list, or document index?

### Promise and point

- Does it make a specific promise about what the reader will get?
- Does it express the thesis or the central question, not merely the opening anecdote?
- Does it undersell the piece, oversell it, or point to a side issue?
- Is the promised payoff actually delivered, and where?

### Parse and economy

- Can a reader understand it on the first pass?
- Are there stacked abstract nouns, bureaucratic phrases, unnecessary preambles, or jargon?
- Would a shorter or more concrete verb make the same point?
- If there is a colon, question mark, pun, or quotation, does it improve understanding rather than compensate for a weak proposition?

### Tone and audience

- Does the tone match the subject’s seriousness and the piece’s voice?
- Is the cleverness legible to the audience, or does it create an extra puzzle?
- Does the headline honor the people or stakes involved?
- Would the writer stand by the headline if the reader never opened the piece?

### Read-forward and ethical test

- Does the headline create a reason to continue without forcing a click through ambiguity?
- Does it hide a crucial fact or create an interpretation the article later retracts?
- Does it exploit fear, shame, outrage, false urgency, or identity pressure?
- Would a reasonable reader feel accurately informed even if they stopped at the headline?

## Provenance-aware headline examples

The examples below are synthetic and are not evidence.

| Weak or risky | Why it fails | Safer direction |
|---|---|---|
| “The shocking truth about assessment” | No subject, no promise, unsupported emotional cue | Name the decision and the evidence boundary |
| “This one number changes everything” | Withholds the number and overstates consequence | State the number, population, and what it changes |
| “Dashboards: Data, equity, and the future” | Topic list with no argument | State what dashboards do under a specified condition |
| “Schools are failing because of data” | Broad causal claim and undefined population | Name the mechanism, setting, and evidence strength |
| “Can this policy save public education?” | Vague question and inflated scale | Ask the bounded policy question the piece can answer |

## Preference and testing discipline

If the medium supports reader testing, test after integrity screening. Record:

- audience sample and context;
- variants shown and order/randomization;
- outcome (understanding, perceived promise, trust, qualified interest, click, completion, or action);
- uncertainty and sample limits;
- whether the “winner” was also accurate and useful.

Do not optimize only for clicks. A title that raises clicks by making readers expect a different story is a failed title even if the metric improves. Do not treat an A/B test as proof of originality, factual correctness, or ethical acceptability.

## Headline-to-opening-to-thesis stamp

For each selected headline, record:

| Element | One-sentence statement |
|---|---|
| Headline promise | A cold reader reasonably expects to learn/feel/decide ___ |
| Opening delivery | The first beat delivers ___ without bait-and-switch |
| Central question | The piece then investigates ___ |
| Thesis answer | The piece argues ___ |
| Evidence path | The claim is supported/qualified by ___ |
| Payoff | The reader leaves understanding/deciding ___ |

If the headline points to a question but the body answers a different question, classify the issue as `DRIFT_REQUIRES_DECISION` rather than editing the headline in isolation.
