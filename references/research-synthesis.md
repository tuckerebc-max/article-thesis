# Research synthesis

Research snapshot: 2026-08-14.

This file records the evidence base used to design the skill. It is a source map and design rationale, not a replacement for the linked sources. The skill should refresh current or disputed claims during each project rather than treating this snapshot as timeless.

## Design conclusions

### 1. A thesis is a bounded claim, not a topic

Harvard’s writing guidance treats a thesis as a central claim or main insight that is arguable, thoughtful, evidence-derived, and appropriately scoped. Its tests ask whether a reasonable reader could disagree, whether the claim is too obvious or too broad, whether it can be supported, and whether it adds understanding. The University of Toronto emphasizes a definite, limited assertion that requires support, while warning that a thesis need not always be one sentence or appear at a fixed location. George Mason adds that a useful thesis helps organize the paper and can evolve during writing.

**Skill implication:** distinguish topic, question, observation, hypothesis, claim, warrant, implication, and recommendation. Require scope and evidence fit. Permit multi-sentence theses when the argument genuinely needs qualification.

Sources:

- [Harvard College Writing Center: Developing a Thesis](https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/thesis)
- [University of Toronto Writing Advice: Thesis Statements](https://advice.writing.utoronto.ca/planning/thesis-statements/)
- [George Mason University Writing Center: Thesis Statements](https://writingcenter.gmu.edu/writing-resources/general-writing-practices/thesis-statements)
- [George Mason University: Two Steps to a Working Thesis Statement](https://writingcenter.gmu.edu/articles/7605)

### 2. Arguments need a visible bridge from evidence to claim

The Toulmin model, as presented in Oklahoma State’s open scientific-writing resource, separates claim, data/grounds, warrant, backing, qualifier, and rebuttal. The warrant is the reasoning bridge; qualifiers tune claim strength; rebuttals force the writer to address a serious alternative. These are practical diagnostic categories, not a rigid order for every genre.

**Skill implication:** every candidate thesis gets a claim type, warrant, evidence set, qualifier, strongest objection, and response. A citation without a valid bridge is not evidence fit.

Source:

- [Oklahoma State University: Argument Structure—The Toulmin Model](https://open.library.okstate.edu/writingforpublication/part/section-1-for-research-argument-argument-structure-the-toulmin-model/)

### 3. Opinion writing adds a public and timely job

Stanford Graduate School of Education’s op-ed guidance asks writers to sharpen the goal, audience, timing, action, outlet fit, new contribution, personal credibility, evidence, links, and anticipated critics. It recommends a strong early entry point and specific solutions, while warning against jargon, generic claims, weak evidence, and untargeted submission.

**Skill implication:** an opinion thesis is not just an academic claim in a warmer voice. It normally needs a public stake, a timely peg, a defined audience, a feasible ask, and a defensible response to the strongest objection.

Source:

- [Stanford GSE: Writing and Pitching Op-Eds](https://ed.stanford.edu/resources/faculty/write-pitch-op-eds)

### 4. Headlines are promises under severe compression

Poynter’s headline checklists emphasize accuracy, out-of-context intelligibility, specific promise, easy parsing, economy, known nouns, explanatory value, tone, and matching the actual point. NPR’s practical guidance similarly emphasizes specificity, ease of understanding, reaction, restraint with cleverness, and fit with the spirit of the story. NPR’s 2025 public-editor account adds a newsroom process safeguard: headlines are reviewed for clarity, accuracy, and tone, and the writer/editor should understand the complete story.

The American Press Institute recommends writing headlines as if they may be the only thing a reader sees, particularly in misinformation contexts. Its research summaries also report that, in examined political-news settings, curiosity/clickbait-style headlines did not reliably outperform traditional summary headlines and sometimes performed worse. These results are context-specific; they do not justify a universal “summary always wins” rule.

**Skill implication:** headline quality has hard integrity gates before preference or engagement tests. A headline is a one-idea public promise that must survive a cold read without the body, image, or brand.

Sources:

- [Poynter: 10 Questions to Help You Write Better Headlines](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2011/10-questions-to-help-you-write-better-headlines/)
- [Poynter: 10 Questions for Your Headline-Writing Checklist](https://www.poynter.org/educators-students/2017/10-questions-for-your-headline-writing-checklist/)
- [NPR: How to Write Great Headlines That Keep Readers Engaged](https://www.vpm.org/npr-news/2015-10-25/how-to-write-great-headlines-that-keep-readers-engaged)
- [NPR Public Editor: The Anatomy of an NPR Headline](https://www.northcountrypublicradio.org/news/npr/g-s1-60750/the-anatomy-of-an-npr-headline)
- [American Press Institute: Talking About Misinformation with First Draft](https://americanpressinstitute.org/talking-about-misinformation-with-first-draft/)
- [American Press Institute: How Geography, Headlines and Fact-Checking Affect Campaign Coverage Engagement](https://americanpressinstitute.org/geography-headlines-fact-checking/)

### 5. Curiosity is useful only when the promise remains faithful

Peer-reviewed headline research distinguishes attention from trustworthy information selection. Scacco and Muddiman’s two-study work compares summary and curiosity headlines and reports that summary headlines can increase perceived information adequacy and expectations of clear information relative to some curiosity headlines. Scott analyzes how clickbait creates an information gap by making a missing referent salient while providing too little content to identify the story. A 2024 *Scientific Reports* study examines when curiosity gaps can backfire as headline concreteness increases.

**Skill implication:** allow tension, surprise, and forward motion, but never use material ambiguity, false implication, or a withheld noun to force a click. Treat curiosity as a rhetorical instrument whose value depends on the actual audience, medium, and delivered payoff.

Sources:

- [Scacco & Muddiman: The Curiosity Effect](https://doi.org/10.1177/1461444819863408)
- [Scott: You Won’t Believe What’s in This Paper!](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pragma.2020.12.023)
- [When Curiosity Gaps Backfire](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-81575-9)
- [Apresjan & Orlov: Pragmatic Mechanisms of Manipulation in Russian Online Media](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pragma.2022.02.003)

### 6. Narrative structure is a comprehension and meaning system

Bruner’s account of narrative treats narrative as a way of constructing and organizing reality, not merely a decorative wrapper. Labov and Waletzky’s structural work, as summarized in later scholarship, identifies orientation, complicating action, evaluation, resolution, and coda as useful analytic units while also warning that narrative is socially situated and not a universal template. Experimental work on causal coherence finds that readers reactivate information when it becomes causally relevant; more recent neuroscience work likewise links subjective comprehension to causal connections among events.

**Skill implication:** select structure for the thesis’s causal, interpretive, or decision logic. Require a real turn, relationship, or consequence. Do not use narrative drama to hide evidence gaps or to turn a single case into a population claim.

Sources:

- [Bruner: The Narrative Construction of Reality](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/448619)
- [Labov: Narrative Theory](https://www.ling.upenn.edu/~wlabov/sfs.html)
- [Causal Coherence and the Availability of Locations and Objects During Narrative Comprehension](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16156181/)
- [Cognitive and Neural State Dynamics of Narrative Comprehension](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8549535/)
- [Introduction: Narrative in Critical Discourse Studies](https://doi.org/10.1080/17405904.2020.1802765)

### 7. The local dependencies supply the workflow spine

The local `$narrative-engine` supplies source inventory, One Thing/Ask/Through-Line/Boundary, arc and overlay selection, Context/Focal/Evidence Fit, the skeleton stamp, provenance tags, and cold-read/evidence gates. `$writing-craft-armature` supplies the controlling “built to prove” test, section service, subtraction, because-joints, and earned conclusion. `$deep-research` supplies source hierarchy, uncertainty labels, adversarial comparison, and citation verification. `$agent-workflow-designer` supplies bounded roles, stable IDs, checkpoints, canonical outputs, and exception handling.

**Skill implication:** thesis development is an upstream decision layer. It does not duplicate Narrative Engine’s full drafting workflow; it hands Narrative Engine a selected, tested thesis plus an evidence and hook map.

## Source-quality and use rules

| Source category | Best use | Required caution |
|---|---|---|
| Peer-reviewed research / DOI | Claims about cognition, persuasion, narrative, headline behavior, or empirical effects | Check design, population, effect, and applicability; do not convert one study into a universal rule |
| Official institutional writing guidance | Practical criteria, genre expectations, teaching heuristics | Treat as guidance rather than an empirical law; preserve genre and disciplinary differences |
| Professional newsroom/editorial guidance | Headline, audience, accuracy, workflow, and ethical practice | Distinguish craft wisdom from measured outcomes; do not copy formulas mechanically |
| Local dependency skills | Workflow interfaces, gates, schemas, and provenance | Reuse contracts; do not silently broaden their authority |
| Search snippets, secondary summaries, social posts | Discovery only | Verify before using; record `UNVERIFIED` until checked |

## Research round protocol for future runs

1. Define the question and the claim type before searching.
2. Search primary/official/peer-reviewed sources first; use institutional guides for operational criteria.
3. Build a source ledger with exact title, author/organization, date, URL/DOI, access date, relevant finding, limitation, and intended use.
4. Seek disconfirming evidence for any thesis that appears unusually persuasive.
5. Separate empirical findings, expert guidance, local design decisions, and illustrative examples.
6. Stamp time-sensitive claims `[as of: YYYY-MM-DD]`.
7. Stop when each material thesis claim has adequate support, the strongest objection has been searched, and new sources are no longer changing the decision; record remaining gaps rather than searching indefinitely.

## What this research does not establish

- There is no universal headline formula that guarantees attention or quality.
- Narrative transportation or curiosity is not a license for manipulation or unsupported persuasion.
- A thesis is not required to be a one-sentence slogan in every discipline or genre.
- A high engagement metric is not proof of accuracy, originality, or reader benefit.
- A polished headline is not proof that the body has one defensible thesis.

## 8. Strong opinion pages converge on an editorial bar, not a single voice

Public guidance from the [Washington Post](https://helpcenter.washingtonpost.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003675788-Submit-an-op-ed), [New York Times](https://thenewyorktimeshelpcenter.helpjuice.com/115014809107-New-York-Times-Opinion-Guest-Essays), [Los Angeles Times](https://www.latimes.com/oe-howtosubmitoped-story.html), [Hechinger Report](https://hechingerreport.org/op-ed-guidelines/), and [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/forbesstaff/article/forbes-editorial-values-and-standards//) converges on clear argument, evidence, freshness or reader value, credible author relation, readable prose, and editorial/ethical discipline. The guidelines differ in length, exclusivity, audience, and process. They should therefore be translated into outlet-fit checks, not imitated as a common style.

The [Financial Times opinion submission page](https://www.ft.com/content/e3e8ff2b-95c7-48f8-9eda-312494422e10) is partly subscription-gated; current submission requirements should be verified live. Its public framing supports a useful design principle: intellectual bite and readable compression are compatible with evidence and civility. A Chicago Tribune direction should likewise be verified against current Tribune rules; secondary descriptions are directional only.

**Skill implication:** define “elite op-ed” as a set of transferable properties—field-moving argument, evidence, clear public consequence, author-specific voice, controlled craft, and publication fit—rather than a simulated outlet voice.

## 9. Distinctive voice requires provenance and deliberate choice

The local `writing-craft`, `writing-craft-armature`, `narrative-engine`, and `anti-ai-slop-writing` skills converge on a practical insight: voice is preserved when editing protects the writer’s meaning, stakes, personality, rhythm, and unusual but purposeful phrasing; it is weakened by generic paraphrase, repetitive cadence, canned transitions, false symmetry, and ungrounded embellishment.

**Skill implication:** create a voiceprint and texture bank before drafting, then run a voice-versus-polish gate. A piece fails if it is clear but interchangeable or vivid but ungrounded.

## 10. Field movement is a reader update

Institutional thesis guidance and Toulmin argument design establish arguability, scope, warrant, evidence, qualifier, and rebuttal. For public writing, those tests need an additional contribution check: what does the piece let an informed reader see, connect, question, or do differently? New wording is not sufficient evidence of novelty.

**Skill implication:** map the current frame, overlooked fact or voice, reconnection/mechanism, evidence delta, reader before/after belief, and bounded Ask before selecting a thesis.

## Research refresh rule

For a live submission or time-sensitive issue, recheck venue rules, current facts, conflicts, and source dates at the publication gate. The benchmark snapshot is not a substitute for current verification.
