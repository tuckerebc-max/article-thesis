# Narrative-structure crosswalk

This is a thesis-selection crosswalk into `$narrative-engine`; it is not a second narrative framework. The local Narrative Engine instructions remain authoritative for source inventory, focal statement, arc definitions, communication overlays, skeleton stamps, provenance, and quality gates.

## Structural premise

A narrative structure is selected because it makes the thesis easier to understand, test, and remember for this audience. It is not selected because a story shape is fashionable or dramatic. The structure must expose the right question, preserve evidence and limits, create a real movement, and land the Ask without inflating the claim.

The local Narrative Engine uses:

- `One Thing`: the single payload the audience should carry;
- `Ask`: what the audience should understand, decide, or do;
- `Through-Line`: the recurring movement or question;
- `Boundary`: what the piece will not claim;
- `Context Fit`, `Focal Fit`, `Evidence Fit`: 1–5 selection scores;
- one narrative arc plus at most one communication overlay;
- a skeleton stamp before drafting;
- a cold-read focal-fidelity gate after drafting.

Disqualify a structure when `Focal Fit < 3` or `Evidence Fit < 3`, regardless of its total score.

## Thesis-to-arc crosswalk

| Thesis situation | Best first structures to test | Why they may fit | Reject when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Genuine contradiction, reframe, or costly rise in status | Prestige | Lets the audience see a promise, deepening complication, turn, and consequence | The “rise” is merely a list of accomplishments or the contradiction is manufactured |
| Real anomaly with competing explanations | Mystery Box | Holds a bounded question while clues revise the initial interpretation | The answer is already known, evidence is too thin, or suspense would hide material risk |
| Strategy, implementation, or operating challenge | Heist | Makes goal, constraints, plan, setback, workaround, and sustainment visible | There is no real strategy, no constraint, or the “crew” is invented |
| Choice under uncertainty with future consequences | Time Machine | Starts from a plausible future or decision point and traces causes and levers back to now | Projection is presented as certainty or no decision is actually available |
| Resistant audience needs a familiar frame before a reframe | Trojan Horse | Uses a familiar premise to introduce a new mental model, then demonstrates limits | The familiar frame is deceptive, the audience’s autonomy is bypassed, or the evidence cannot carry the reframe |
| Transformation with an identifiable actor and cost | Hero’s Journey | Can show departure, resistance, ordeal, change, and return | The work is impersonal, routine, or the “hero” framing erases systems and affected people |
| Genuine conflict escalating toward a consequence | Freytag | Makes rising action, turn, climax, and aftermath legible | There is no dramatic escalation or drama would distort a complex issue |
| Outcome is known; mechanism or failure needs explanation | Columbo | Begins with the outcome and re-reads the small details that explain it | The causal explanation is speculative or the outcome is not established |
| Repeated pattern across moments or cases | Game of the Scene | Names a recurring interaction/game, heightens it, and reveals the mechanism | The pattern is anecdotal, the mechanism is absent, or repetition becomes padding |
| Legitimate competing perspectives need integration | Rashomon | Fairly presents views, identifies conflict/missing dimension, and builds an integrative frame | False balance obscures evidence, safety, rights, or a necessary recommendation |
| No arc passes | Direct fallback | `Answer → Why → Evidence → Limits/Risks → Action/Next question` keeps the thesis visible | The material genuinely needs a different form or should be split |

The table is a routing aid. The selected structure still needs an actual beat-level stamp.

## Claim-to-structure logic

Before scoring an arc, write the thesis’s movement in one of these forms:

- `misreading → evidence → reframe → consequence`;
- `anomaly → competing explanation → discriminating evidence → answer/limit`;
- `goal → constraint → strategy → setback → adaptation → result`;
- `future consequence → present causes → decision fork → levers and uncertainty`;
- `familiar frame → resistance → new model → demonstration → limits`;
- `outcome → baseline explanation → mechanism/detail → re-read → implication`;
- `perspective A/B/C → conflict → missing dimension → qualified integration`.

If none describes the argument without forcing it, use the direct fallback or revise the thesis. This test prevents the arc from dictating a thesis the evidence does not support.

- [ ] The opening leads to the selected central question.
- [ ] Every protected beat advances, tests, complicates, or limits the thesis.
- [ ] The turn is evidence-backed and not merely tonal.
- [ ] The payoff is no stronger than the evidence map.
- [ ] The close delivers the Ask without importing a new thesis.
- [ ] The structure works at the actual length without padding.
- [ ] The arc does not hide risk, uncertainty, or counterevidence.

## Overlay selection

Use no more than one communication overlay. Choose it only when it clarifies audience movement:

- `Answer → Why → Evidence → Limits → Action` for direct explanatory or decision writing;
- `Problem → Stakes → Options → Recommendation` for policy and executive choices;
- `Question → Evidence → Reframe → Implication` for analytical work;
- `Before → Friction → Adaptation → After` for implementation or case-based learning;
- `Claim → Warrant → Evidence → Counterargument → Qualified conclusion` for explicit argument.

Do not pile multiple overlays onto one arc. If each beat has three labels, the writer probably has a structural ambiguity to resolve.

## Decision rules

- Prefer the simplest structure that makes the thesis legible.
- Preserve chronological order only when it helps causality, discovery, or accountability; otherwise use a more intelligible order.
- Make a “mystery” from a real information gap, not from withholding a known fact from the reader.
- Use case material as an entry point, not as a silent substitute for population evidence.
- Put counterevidence where it changes the reader’s understanding, not only in a late disclaimer.
- If the audience’s main decision is the payoff, do not bury it behind a decorative arc.
- If the structure depends on a future scenario, mark assumptions and uncertainty at the moment they matter.
- If the thesis changes, re-stamp the structure; do not leave the old arc in place by inertia.

## Completion condition

A structure is ready to draft only when a cold reader who sees the beat list can identify:

1. what the piece is trying to prove or establish;
2. what question or tension moves it forward;
3. where evidence changes or qualifies the audience’s model;
4. what the final implication or Ask will be;
5. what the piece deliberately does not claim.
