# Exemplar screening

The skill studies examples to learn transferable decisions, not to imitate surface style. Academic and opinion exemplars should be sampled, reconstructed, compared, and tested against their evidence and audience rather than admired in isolation.

## Sampling plan

For a normal project, screen at least:

- 2 academic or research exemplars in the relevant discipline or adjacent discipline;
- 2 opinion or public-facing exemplars aimed at a similar audience;
- 1 counterexample whose headline, thesis, structure, or evidence fit fails;
- 1 exemplar chosen for evidence/argument quality even if its voice or subject is different.

For a high-stakes or large project, expand the sample until new examples stop changing the candidate criteria. Record the stopping reason. Do not select only successful or famous pieces; survivorship bias hides failure modes.

## Source screen

Before analysis, record:

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Source ID | Stable identifier such as `EX-A01` |
| Title/headline | Exact title, linked; do not reproduce long text |
| Author / organization | Provenance and relevant authority |
| Date / version | Publication and access date |
| Genre / medium | Article, paper, op-ed, talk, report, etc. |
| Audience | Intended or inferred audience, with evidence |
| Evidence base | Primary, secondary, observed, cited, anecdotal, or mixed |
| Accessibility | Full text, excerpt, abstract, or metadata only |
| Fit | Similarity to current project and what differs |
| Integrity | Any mismatch, overclaim, correction, or unresolved issue |

If only a title or abstract is available, do not reconstruct a full thesis as fact. Mark the card `PARTIAL` and lower confidence.

## Academic screen

Look for:

- a specific research or interpretive question;
- a claim that is more than a topic or summary;
- a visible relation among evidence, warrant, and conclusion;
- an appropriate relationship between design and claim strength;
- clear boundaries around case, sample, construct, period, or text;
- an explicit or recoverable contribution;
- serious treatment of alternative interpretations, limitations, or counterevidence;
- structure that makes the reasoning inspectable.

Do not equate technical density with rigor. A thesis can be complex and still be unclear, overbroad, or under-supported.

Useful academic guidance for screening criteria:

- [Harvard College Writing Center: Developing a Thesis](https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/thesis)
- [University of Toronto Writing Advice: Thesis Statements](https://advice.writing.utoronto.ca/planning/thesis-statements/)
- [George Mason University Writing Center: Thesis Statements](https://writingcenter.gmu.edu/writing-resources/general-writing-practices/thesis-statements)
- [Oklahoma State University: Argument Structure—The Toulmin Model](https://open.library.okstate.edu/writingforpublication/part/section-1-for-research-argument-argument-structure-the-toulmin-model/)

## Opinion-piece screen

Look for:

- a public proposition rather than a private reaction;
- a timely reason to care and a defined target outlet/audience;
- an opening that earns attention without fabrication or theatrical inflation;
- an original contribution, interpretation, comparison, or recommendation;
- specific action or a choice the audience can realistically consider;
- evidence and credibility appropriate to the argument;
- preemption of the strongest critique without false balance;
- a close that returns to the point and leaves the audience with a credible consequence.

Useful opinion guidance:

- [Stanford GSE: Writing and Pitching Op-Eds](https://ed.stanford.edu/resources/faculty/write-pitch-op-eds)
- [Poynter: Six Questions That Can Help Journalists Find a Focus and Tell Better Stories](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2011/6-questions-that-can-help-journalists-find-a-focus-tell-better-stories/)
- [NPR: How to Write Great Headlines That Keep Readers Engaged](https://www.vpm.org/npr-news/2015-10-25/how-to-write-great-headlines-that-keep-readers-engaged)

## Comparative synthesis

After cards are complete, compare examples on the same dimensions:

| Dimension | Academic exemplar | Opinion exemplar | Transfer question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Central proposition |  |  | What remains a defensible claim in this project? |
| Scope |  |  | What boundary is needed here? |
| Warrant |  |  | Which inference must be made explicit? |
| Evidence |  |  | What kind and amount of support are required? |
| Counterargument |  |  | What objection should be surfaced early? |
| Headline/title |  |  | Does the public promise match the argument? |
| Hook |  |  | What entry point is honest and audience-fit? |
| Structure |  |  | Which movement could Narrative Engine support? |
| Payoff |  |  | What will the reader be able to understand or decide? |
| Failure mode |  |  | What should this project avoid? |

The synthesis should produce principles such as:

- “Make the mechanism visible before widening the implication.”
- “Use a case to expose the problem, then earn the generalization with separate evidence.”
- “State the public Ask before the reader has to infer why the argument matters.”
- “Let a title name the actual point; let the hook supply texture.”

Each principle needs at least one supporting exemplar and one limitation. Do not turn one successful example into a universal rule.

## Exemplar integrity checks

- Was the piece’s success measured, or is it merely assumed from reputation?
- Does its audience have knowledge, trust, and constraints unlike the current audience?
- Is its evidence available and still current?
- Is its headline optimized for a different medium?
- Does its structure depend on a charismatic writer, exclusive access, or facts the current project lacks?
- Does the piece simplify an objection because the genre permits it, or because the writer avoided it?
- Could adopting the visible style import an invisible overclaim?

## Copyright and attribution

Store links, short paraphrases, and analytical notes. Do not copy full articles, long passages, or proprietary examples into the skill. Quote only when necessary for analysis and keep any excerpt legally and operationally minimal. A reconstructed thesis should be labeled as reconstruction, not presented as the author’s exact wording.
